# Address resolution during migration

A member's IEEE address and endpoint identify its MQTT route. A short address in an imported interview records where that device was seen at the time. The bridge starts each radio session with an empty address map. A miss in the adapter address table says nothing by itself about membership or reachability.

## Coordinator startup

After the Ember adapter reports NETWORK_UP and the bridge verifies the coordinator identity, it enables the high-RAM concentrator and reschedules many-to-one route discovery. Ember returns the delay to the next broadcast. If rescheduling reports discovery disabled (0xffffffff), the bridge explicitly enables discovery and requires a finite delay before declaring radio readiness. Each startup walk uses the same single-probe scheduler as regular availability checks. A recent inbound message can satisfy a queued check. Probes are spaced at least two seconds apart after the preceding probe finishes.

New Z2M imports preserve router status. Native interviews preserve the receiver-on-when-idle bit from the device announcement. The walk includes imported routers and confirmed always-on receivers, including when periodic availability is disabled globally. An explicit per-device availability opt-out excludes that member. Historical imports without router or receiver evidence retain their existing monitoring behaviour; restaging from Z2M recovers the router classification. Sleepy and unclassified devices acquire no additional startup probe.

When a Basic-cluster probe lacks a current short address, it shares the bounded ZDO resolver with controls. A correlated address reply can establish the route for the read. Only the target's own traffic can answer the probe. A leave, expiry or failed submission retires the pending work, and an unresolved address supplies no offline evidence.

## Ordinary reports and controls

- For an incoming report, EZSP 19 can supply the source IEEE address when the APS source-EUI64 bit is set. Otherwise the bridge asks the adapter. For an unsolicited observation, a verified mapping survives silence for the current radio session. If identity is still unknown, the bridge sends a unicast `IEEE_addr_req` to the observed short address. Eligible unsolicited reports, alarms and remote gestures can wait on that reverse query. Its bounded per-sender queue preserves arrival order and suppresses copies with the same APS identity and payload. Direct source evidence or a correlated address response naming a registered member releases that member's queue. A fresh admission, conflicting assignment, timeout or shutdown discards it.
- For a control, the bridge asks the adapter for the member's current short address. If that misses, the bridge checks for an unexpired session mapping, then issues a `NWK_addr_req` for the IEEE address to receivers awake when idle. A held command keeps its original IEEE address, endpoint, MQTT route generation where available, and deadline. The bridge plans it again before submission. A delivery timeout after submission never enters this discovery queue.
- The resolver checks the response type, transaction sequence, subject address, registered IEEE address and outstanding deadline. A device or a neighbour answering for it can return a successful single-device response. The IEEE and short address in the payload identify the subject. The APS sender and optional source EUI identify the respondent. An accepted trust-centre admission can establish the pair during an interview. Device announcements must agree with their source short and any advertised source EUI. A contradictory observation invalidates the association. A hit in the adapter table cannot clear a disputed short address.
- Simultaneous misses for one address share a query. The bridge admits 32 probes and 32 held controls, reserves half the probe capacity for forward lookups and allows one peer to hold up to 16 controls. It spaces probes by at least 250 ms. A fresh session waits eight seconds before issuing address-discovery transactions. Confirmed adapter hits and directly verified incoming traffic can proceed immediately. A new cold miss can wait up to 16 seconds including startup and discovery. An existing caller deadline still applies. The probe cooldown is 16 seconds. The five-minute deadline limits outbound routing and transaction leases. A cached sensor report or button action cannot extend the outbound route. A confirmed rejoin creates a new assignment even at the same short address. A leave, conflict or loss of the coordinator session invalidates the associated input identity. A peer reboot that produces no observable identity change cannot be inferred from silence.
- A short-address conflict retires that assignment. A response to an older query cannot establish ownership of that short. Queries for other addresses can still finish. A neighbour’s reply cannot clear a disputed address. Conflicting successful replies to the same exchange invalidate the previously accepted neighbour mapping while that exchange is tracked. Requests requiring a timely reply, including Poll Control check-ins and Tuya time requests, can use a current verified mapping immediately. They never wait in the observation queue; discovery can help the next request.

ZDO address discovery is specified in sections 2.4.3.1.1–2 and 2.4.4.2.1–2. Silicon Labs documents the source-EUI flag and the host's responsibility to discontinue use of a conflicted short address. [Zigbee R23](https://csa-iot.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/05-3474-23-csg-zigbee-specification-compressed.pdf) · [EZSP incoming packet fields](https://docs.silabs.com/zigbee/latest/sisdk-ezsp-reference-guide/03-protocol-format) · [EZSP conflict callback](https://docs.silabs.com/zigbee/8.2.2/sisdk-ezsp-reference-guide/08-messaging-frames)

IAS status notifications and status reports follow the observation path. Enrollment, keypad commands, lock traffic and OTA keep their independent identity checks. A held sensor observation cannot complete a radio transaction or update the security panel. Occupancy and zone-status transitions use ordered MQTT snapshots.

Ordinary setting readbacks, thermostat schedule steps and fast-poll stops keep their original destination lease. A fresh adapter lookup may reveal a contradiction. An empty lookup leaves the existing lease in force, subject to revocation, expiry, queued lifecycle callbacks and the final unicast check. A bind reply still requires independent confirmation of its sender; an unverifiable reply leaves the outcome indeterminate.

## Distributed routing and sensor delivery

| Traffic | What the coordinator can conclude |
| --- | --- |
| Address discovery | A neighbour can answer a network- or IEEE-address query for an end device from its neighbour table. Use the address pair in the payload for ordinary routing and buffered observations. The neighbour's reply alone supplies no evidence of the child's availability. [Zigbee R23, §§2.4.3.1.1.2 and 2.4.3.1.2.2](https://csa-iot.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/05-3474-23-csg-zigbee-specification-compressed.pdf) |
| Endpoint discovery | The bridge directs its Active Endpoints and Simple Descriptor queries to the device. Check the selected responder, transaction and address of interest. An interview keeps the current admitted assignment across an adapter table miss and checks it again before transmitting. The specification also allows a client to select a discovery cache as its query destination. [Zigbee R23, §§2.4.3.1.5–6](https://csa-iot.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/05-3474-23-csg-zigbee-specification-compressed.pdf) |
| Sensor and remote input | A registered endpoint can deliver an unsolicited attribute report to a group. Supported ordinary readings delivered by the adapter to a local endpoint may update MQTT. They have no unicast waiter to complete. Cold identity discovery can also hold supported unsolicited Tuya datapoint reports. A time request, command response or enrollment request needs its own handling. |
| Network routing | Ember maintains concentrator routes, neighbours and relay paths. The host checks the route-discovery scheduler and probes eligible awake devices. A relay or route error supplies no application identity. The bridge does not collect remote routing tables, so its logs alone cannot establish every hop. [Silicon Labs messaging reference](https://docs.silabs.com/zigbee/latest/sisdk-ezsp-reference-guide/08-messaging-frames) |

## Behavior at each boundary

| Boundary | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Address ownership | A destination carries its IEEE address, short address, radio session and an opaque assignment token. A confirmed rejoin at the same short creates a new token. Consistent observations preserve the current token. A delayed leave identifying an old pair cannot evict its replacement. [Resolver](src/bridge/addresses.rs) |
| Before transmission | Managed unicast checks the assignment and already decoded callbacks immediately before submitting to the serial worker. Ordinary multi-frame controls return to the radio loop after each frame. A conflict can retire the remaining frames while unrelated peers continue. The direct IEEE-address discovery probe is a separate, payload-checked operation. [Submission](src/ash/destination.rs) · [Serial handoff](src/ash.rs) |
| Replies and follow-ups | Pending application and management work keeps its original destination. An MQTT follow-up also keeps the admitting route generation, including when its name is reused. The bridge checks the protocol fields available on the reply. Ordinary controls, weekly schedules and their follow-ups allocate from slots clear of active transactions and recent matching requests. The per-session wire history lasts 16 seconds. Protected responses retain the initiating assignment and their existing authorization checks. [Radio loop](src/bridge/ember.rs) · [Management](src/bridge/admin.rs) |
| Interviews | The radio task rechecks the assignment before accepting a completed interview. The standalone interview checks current device identity and retains that assignment through handover to the bridge. Historical short addresses supply discovery hints. [Standalone interview](src/interview.rs) · [Joining worker](src/bridge/joining.rs) |
| Enrollment and availability | An IAS attempt starts when the adapter accepts its first inspection request. A lookup miss or adapter refusal backs off, and a verified peer sighting can wake enrollment. Availability counts accepted probes against a single current assignment. An address lookup miss provides no evidence that the device ignored a probe. [Enrollment](src/bridge/enrollment.rs) · [Availability](src/availability/tracker.rs) |
| Restart and MQTT | Confirmed departures survive restart in redb. Confirmed readmission permits the route again. Interrupted tagged management requests retain an indeterminate result when an effect may have occurred. Retrying that identifier returns its recorded result. Ordinary batch interruption reports the count accepted for submission, the uncertain step and the unsent suffix while the MQTT route is current. [Transaction journal](src/mqtt/admin/journal.rs) · [Route ownership](src/mqtt.rs) |

An undetected reassignment can still look like a valid adapter lookup. The host can only act on identity evidence it receives. It cannot recall a frame after serial handoff, infer the physical effect of an adapter acknowledgement, or distinguish identical replies once wire identifiers are reused outside the correlation window. Wire history for application commands is session-local. An ordinary untagged MQTT command independently published again is a new request. ZDO discovery increments its own transaction counter through wrap. The other endpoint-zero command producers still have independent counters, so this work does not establish whole-endpoint sequence conformance.

## Regression evidence

The [MQTT and ASH round-trip test](tests/bridge_roundtrip.rs) imports old addresses for its simulated lamps. The adapter refuses forward lookup for one lamp and reverse lookup for another. The fixture checks both ZDO requests, their destination and payload, rejects application sends to historical addresses and rejects duplicate OFF commands. For the reverse miss it emits an initial brightness value of 240, answers discovery and then reports 4. The independent MQTT subscriber requires the expected brightness for that lamp. The test runs on both EZSP 13 and 19 with Mosquitto and a Linux pseudo-terminal.

The cold-observation fixture forces reverse lookup misses and has a router answer for the sensor and button. Its independent MQTT subscriber checks two ordered occupancy transitions, a single button action despite a retransmission, and a group-addressed illuminance report. The startup-walk fixture exercises the opposite direction: the router identifies the target, then the bridge reads that target's Basic cluster. The interview fixture evicts an adapter entry between descriptor steps and interleaves unrelated descriptor errors with the genuine replies.

The resolver unit tests cover response correlation, proxy and unknown-member replies, bounded queues, duplicate probes, expiry, command ordering, membership changes and contradictory adapter evidence. A two-cluster bind fixture injects a conflict after the first submission and checks that the second frame stays unsent. A separate device then finishes its own bind. Backup and restore retain the interrupted operation’s status. A transport fixture inserts a conflict during a successful lookup and observes the unaffected peer as the next serial submission. The serial peer is simulated. Physical parent/child discovery and conflict timing remain live-radio test cases.

## Original implementation plan

The following review plan records the pre-change findings and the acceptance criteria used for this implementation.

The unit of review is one effect: submitting a frame, accepting a reply, writing an interview result, or publishing state. For each, record the intended IEEE and endpoint, the source of its address, what can invalidate it, the last point the host controls, and the outcome if interrupted there. The plan covers the managed Ember bridge, EZSP 13/19, the standalone interview entry point, migration and restart. RF routing inside the adapter and silent identity changes that the host has no evidence of are outside the host's guarantee.

### Findings that determine patch order

- The registry already has an opaque membership token and checks both token identity and reverse address ownership in `Registry::resolve`. The serving loop also creates a separate session-local `Addresses`. A further independent generation counter in every consumer would recreate the invalidation problem. Review the registry and resolver together. [Registry](src/registry.rs) · [Radio loop](src/bridge/ember.rs) · [Resolver](src/bridge/addresses.rs)
- An explicit conflict takes an early return before management observes the event. A contradiction found by `Addresses::check_adapter` also retires mappings, but returns a boolean to its caller. Both paths must reach the same invalidation mechanism. Admission, a duplicate announcement, address change and departure have different meanings. An address conflict alone does not establish that either device left. [Resolver](src/bridge/addresses.rs) · [Management](src/bridge/admin.rs)
- The managed interview already checks its saved short against the adapter. ASH can queue a callback during that check, before the subsequent raw send. Timer-driven management, presence and Poll Control also run before the main callback selection, and application batches send multiple frames per turn. Changing which branch wins a single select cannot cover all of these. [Interview dispatch](src/bridge/admin.rs) · [EZSP exchange](src/ash/v13.rs) · [Radio loop](src/bridge/ember.rs)
- In the diagnostic CLI, failed forward and reverse lookups followed by an unanswered identity probe can still lead to an interview using the previous short. Its observations are labelled `LastKnownAddress` and the bindings loader rejects them. That protects import, but the diagnostic still directs device requests to an unverified destination. [Standalone probe](src/interview.rs) · [CLI entry point](src/bin/zigbee-bridge/command.rs) · [Import check](src/bridge/bindings.rs)

### Closing the review

For each production send and completion, identify which of patches 1–7 establishes its invariant or explain why it is exempt. Re-enumerate direct EZSP sends, helpers and queued continuations after the refactor. Review invalidation immediately before lookup, during lookup, after lookup but before handoff, between batch frames, after adapter acceptance, and just before completion is persisted. Repeat the analysis for a changed short, a rejoin at the same short, a replacement under the same MQTT name and a new radio session. Under queue pressure, account for deadlines, drops, unrelated-device progress and release of abandoned waiters.

Use the existing pseudo-terminal/MQTT harness to protect representative boundaries: a conflict between two bind frames, A→B→A reuse with a late completion, and a cold IAS table followed by a check-in. Preserve the independent serial and MQTT observations. The physical bench is useful for parent-assisted discovery and adapter-specific ordering. A local token cannot disambiguate two identical wire replies after identifier reuse, or recall work already accepted by the adapter. Record those limits when specifying a retry policy.
